First, the mismatch

Your result

The Consumer Room

Your buyer is the person using the product, and she is paying with her own money. That means every post has to do two jobs at once: prove the thing works, and prove it works for someone like her specifically. Most FemTech content aimed at consumers does the first and skips the second, which is why engagement looks healthy while conversion does not.

The fix is not more content. It is content that removes personal risk: specific outcomes, specific people, specific circumstances, and a visible next step that costs almost nothing to take.

Your result

The Subscriber Room

Your buyer already said yes once. The decision you are actually influencing is the one they make silently every month, which is whether to keep paying. That is a different content problem from acquisition, and it is the one most founders never build for.

Content in this room has to make ongoing value visible outside the product. If the only place a subscriber sees the benefit is inside the app, the renewal is decided by memory, and memory is not on your side.

Your result

The Enterprise Room

Your buyer is not the person using the product. It is a health system director, an employer benefits lead, or a payer CMO, and they are making a procurement decision with their own reputation attached. Consumer-style content does not reach them, and when it does, it does not help them make the case internally.

This is where Pilot Purgatory comes from: real pilots, real enthusiasm, no conversion. The content has to arm someone inside that organisation to argue for you in a room you will never be in.
